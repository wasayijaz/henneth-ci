Most guides to the Pakistan Stock Exchange still tell you that trades settle on a **T+2** basis.
That has been wrong since **9 February 2026**.

The PSX moved to a **T+1 settlement cycle** on that date. If you buy shares today, they are
credited to your account the next trading day. If you sell, your money is available the next
trading day. One session, not two.

This sounds like a technicality. It is not. Settlement timing decides whether you qualify for a
dividend, when you can reuse your money, and how long your broker carries the risk of your trade.
Getting it wrong by one day is how people miss payouts they thought they had earned.

## What settlement actually means

There are two separate moments in every share transaction, and confusing them is the root of most
of the confusion here.

**The trade** is when your buy or sell is matched with someone else's order on the exchange. This
is instant during market hours, and it is the moment your price is fixed.

**The settlement** is when the exchange of ownership and money is finalised — shares moved into
your CDC account, money moved out of it, or the reverse. This happens later.

"T+1" names the gap. **T** is the trade day. **+1** is one trading day after it. So a trade
executed on Monday settles on Tuesday. A trade executed on Thursday settles on Friday.

Note that these are **trading days**, not calendar days. A trade on Friday settles on Monday,
because the market is closed over the weekend. A public holiday pushes it further. The PSX
publishes a trading calendar, and settlement follows it.

## What changed, and when

Until 9 February 2026, the PSX used **T+2** — two trading days between the trade and its
settlement. On that date it moved to **T+1**, halving the gap.

This is recorded in the PSX Regulations, in the version dated 9 February 2026. Clause 2.4(lxxvii)
now defines a Ready Market Contract as one ready for settlement on a T+1 cycle, and Chapter 10,
which governs clearing and settlement, matches throughout. The change was approved by the SECP in
October 2025 with a deliberate lead time so brokers and the clearing infrastructure could prepare.
The National Clearing Company of Pakistan (NCCPL), which actually operates the settlement system,
issued its own circular to clearing members ahead of the switch.

Pakistan is not unusual in doing this. The United States, Canada, Mexico and China had already
moved to shorter cycles; the UK, Switzerland and the EU are scheduled to follow. Shorter
settlement reduces the window in which either side of a trade can fail to deliver, which is the
main argument for it.

## What it changes for you

**Your money comes back a day sooner.** Sell on Monday under the old cycle and the proceeds were
available Wednesday. Now they are available Tuesday. If you sell one position to buy another, you
wait one session instead of two.

**Your shares arrive a day sooner.** The same shift in the other direction.

**Dividend eligibility moves.** This is the one that costs people money, and it gets its own
section below.

**Less counterparty risk.** Between trade and settlement, each side is exposed to the other
failing to deliver. Halving that window halves the exposure. You will probably never notice this
directly — it is mostly a benefit to the clearing system — but it is the reason regulators push
for it.

## The part that catches people out: dividends

To receive a dividend, you must be on the company's register of shareholders when the book closes.
You do not get there by buying on the last day. You get there by buying early enough that your
purchase has **settled** before the register is taken.

That is why the **ex-dividend date** exists. Buy on or after the ex-date and the trade will not
settle in time, so the dividend belongs to the seller, not you. Buy before it, and it is yours.

Because the ex-date is derived from the settlement cycle, **shortening the cycle moves it**. Under
T+2 you needed to buy earlier than you do under T+1. Any guide, spreadsheet or rule of thumb
written before February 2026 that tells you the last safe day to buy is now off by one session.

If you are buying specifically to capture a dividend, check the ex-date on the company's own
announcement rather than working backwards from a rule you remember. The gap between "I own the
shares" and "I own the dividend" is exactly one settlement cycle, and that number changed this
year.

## What did not change

**Negotiated deals still settle differently.** The T+1 cycle applies to the Ready Market — ordinary
buying and selling through the continuous auction, which is what almost every retail investor
uses. Separately negotiated transactions settle on their own terms.

**Your broker's internal timing is their own.** T+1 is when settlement completes at the clearing
level. When funds appear as withdrawable in your particular broker's app, and how quickly a
withdrawal reaches your bank, are matters of that broker's process. The regulated cycle is the
floor, not a promise about any one platform.

**The trading calendar still governs.** T+1 counts trading days. Weekends and public holidays push
settlement out, and Ramadan hours change session times without changing the cycle itself.

## How to check any claim about this yourself

Settlement rules are published, not folklore. The PSX Regulations are on psx.com.pk under the
legal framework section, with the version date on the cover — check that date first, because an
older copy will still say T+2 and will still look official. Chapter 10 covers clearing and
settlement. NCCPL publishes its circulars to clearing members separately.

If a source about PSX settlement does not carry a date, treat it as unreliable. As of writing,
most of the freely available material on this topic still describes a cycle the exchange stopped
using in February 2026 — which is a useful reminder that "widely repeated" and "currently true"
are different things.
