# T+1 settlement — global evidence

Verified 21 July 2026. Companion to [PSX_MECHANICS_VERIFIED.md](PSX_MECHANICS_VERIFIED.md), which
covers the PSX rules themselves. This file covers what happened in markets that moved first, and
is the evidence base for the pillar article.

VERIFIED = institutional source (regulator, exchange, CSD/CCP, index provider). REPORTED = press.

## Claims that are WRONG — do not publish

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Pakistan is one of only ~8 T+1 countries" | **Unsubstantiable.** No body (BIS, IOSCO, WFE, MSCI, FTSE) publishes a census. Traces to an NCCPL official's remark to press. "Fewer than ten" is defensible; eight is not a fact. |
| "T+1 helps Pakistan's MSCI/FTSE reclassification" | **Refuted.** Neither provider scores cycle *length*. MSCI's June 2026 Framework never uses the phrase "settlement cycle". |
| "T+1 improved Pakistan's MSCI standing" | **Refuted by MSCI's own June 2026 review** — noted descriptively, rating unchanged at the bottom band. |
| "Saudi / Gulf markets are on T+1" | **False.** Tadawul T+2 since 2017; DFM and ADX T+2. No Gulf announcement found. |
| "Settlement fails rose after the US move" | **Refuted.** US held 2-3%; Canada's OSC found no significant change over Jan 2021 to Jun 2025. |
| "T+1 broke FX for foreign investors" | **Not supported.** CLS volumes *rose* post-transition; ESMA found no materialised negative impact. |
| "DTCC clearing fund fell $4bn" | Wrong number. DTCC's own figure is **$3.0bn / 23%**. |
| "T+1 cut CCP margin by 41%" | Mislabelled. 41% was a **2021 ex-ante simulation of one component**; the outcome was 23%. |
| "China is on T+1" | Needs nuance or it is wrong: securities settle **T+0**, cash **T+1**. MSCI treats it as an accessibility *problem*. |
| "PSX turnover fell 30.9% after T+1" | Unsourceable, and no source attributes it to T+1. |

## Adoption dates — VERIFIED

| Market | Effective |
|---|---|
| India | Phased 25 Feb 2022, completed 27 Jan 2023 |
| Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Jamaica | 27 May 2024 |
| United States | 28 May 2024 (SEC Release 34-96930) |
| **Pakistan** | **9 Feb 2026** — independently confirmed by MSCI 2026 Review, p.19 |
| Nigeria | **1 June 2026** (CSCS and MSCI; several trackers wrongly say 29 May) |
| UK, EU, Switzerland | **11 Oct 2027** — EU already law via Regulation (EU) 2025/2075 |
| Hong Kong | Consulted Apr 2026, indicative Q4 2027, excludes Northbound Stock Connect |

Moving the *other* way, useful colour: BRVM went T+3 to T+2 in Dec 2025; Oman moves to T+2 in
Sept 2026. Most of the frontier world is still working toward T+2.

**India's T+0 is optional and effectively unused** — 126 trades totalling INR 8,73,762 across both
exchanges in five months (SEBI board memo, Nov 2024). Mandatory broker readiness was deferred
indefinitely on 30 Oct 2025. Do not describe India as "moving to T+0".

## Outcomes — VERIFIED

**Benefits, quantified**
- **US NSCC clearing fund down $3.0bn (23%)**, $12.8bn to $9.8bn — T+1 After Action Report
  (SIFMA/ICI/DTCC), 12 Sep 2024.
- **India NSE Clearing daily margin fell from ~INR 12,000 crore (FY22) to ~INR 4,000 crore** —
  SEBI Board Memorandum, Nov 2024. The best single number for a Pakistani audience.
- RBI cut the intraday risk weighting on custodian-bank Irrevocable Payment Commitments from 50%
  to 30% as a direct consequence of India's move.

**The predicted disasters did not happen**
- **US CNS fails 2.12%** (Jul 2024), "consistent with T+2 settlement rates"; non-CNS 3.31%.
- **Canada's OSC**, analysing TMX CDS data Jan 2021 to Jun 2025: *"no significant change in the
  average proportion of traded securities with fails after T+1 settlement was introduced."* ETFs —
  the segment everyone expected to break — held up *"contrary to the initial concerns"*.
- **CLS average daily value rose** from $6.98tn pre-transition to $7.6tn after — *"little to no
  notable impact"*. ESMA: *"Nothing seems to indicate the materialisation of any negative impact
  on FX markets."*
- India fail data: **not published**. If asked, the honest answer is unknown.

**Costs, and where they landed**
- **ESMA "Thursday effect"** — US-invested EU ETFs showed widened premiums to NAV. ESMA calls it a
  *"moderate negative impact"* and says further analysis is needed. **Do not overstate.**
- **APAC operational load** (ESMA, Nov 2024): errors *"handled in the middle of the night in
  Japan"*, shifts adjusted to cover Asia evening hours, custodian cut-offs brought forward.
- **BIS Quarterly Review, June 2026** on PvP cut-offs and rising same-day FX needs — the strongest
  forward-risk citation available.
- **ASIFMA to SEBI, 12 Jan 2024** — the single best line for the article: T+2 to T+1 was
  uncomfortable but absorbable for foreign investors; T+1 to T+0 *"will require a complete
  overhaul"* because *"FPIs will have to pre-fund at least one or two days before due to time zone
  differences."*

## Index classification — the key finding

MSCI's Market Classification Framework (June 2026) scores *"Delivery Versus Payment (DVP), the
absence of pre-funding requirements/practices and the possibility to use overdrafts"*.
**The phrase "settlement cycle" does not appear anywhere in the document.**

- **Pakistan remains Frontier.** Clearing and Settlement stays in the lowest band. MSCI's stated
  reasons (p.53): *"Omnibus structures are not available. Direct overdraft facilities remain
  prohibited."* **T+1 addresses neither.**
- MSCI noted the move (p.19) in a purely descriptive paragraph, with no rating change.
- **Israel is the clean proof.** Upgraded in 2026 because *"The T+1 settlement cycle is no longer
  an outlier characteristic, as Developed Markets have broadly adopted or are in the process of
  adopting this cycle."* It ran T+1 for years and was penalised for it until peers converged.
  **Providers reward alignment, not speed.**
- MSCI on the general risk (p.15): *"shorter settlement cycles should not introduce further
  operational challenges and risks, such as pre-funding requirements."*
- **FTSE** *does* have a criterion titled "Settlement Cycle (DvP)" — so "not a criterion" would be
  wrong for FTSE. But it is required identically at all four tiers, and T+2 markets including
  Greece (being promoted to Developed) all PASS. FTSE flags pre-funding negatively too (South
  Korea, a Developed market).
- Pakistan is **Frontier** on FTSE, not on the Watch List. Next annual review Sept 2026.
- MSCI 2026 Classification Review: **Pakistan not mentioned** — no reclassification, no watchlist.

## Pakistan post-implementation — the live story, REPORTED only

- **SECP opened a post-implementation consultation** (Mar 2026) covering implementation challenges,
  liquidity, investor participation, operational readiness and increased capital requirements.
- Reported friction: limited banking hours during Ramadan, cheque-clearance delays creating funding
  mismatches, brokers absorbing short-term liquidity gaps at their own risk.
- A Business Recorder headline indicates **PSBA reservations and a recommendation to revert to
  T+2** — ⚠️ the article body returned 403 and **was never read**. Headline-level only. Get a
  subscriber copy before printing this.
- ⚠️ PSX's own 10 Feb 2026 media release was confirmed to exist but **its text was never read**.
  NCCPL's press release and T+1 page return 403 to every route tried. **Do not quote either as if
  read.**
- FPI context, not causation: net outflow of ~$736m in FY26 while the KSE-100 rose ~40%. Cited
  drivers are index reclassification and Middle East risk aversion — **T+1 is not named as a cause**
  by the source.

## The honest through-line

1. T+1 delivered a **real but modest** CCP margin saving without the predicted settlement-fails
   blow-up in either the US or Canada.
2. The genuine costs landed **asymmetrically on foreign and cross-border investors** — funding
   gaps, the Thursday effect, Asia-hours night shifts, pre-funding pressure — not on the domestic
   market that made the change. That is the honest warning for a market courting foreign flows.
3. **Neither MSCI nor FTSE rewards a shorter cycle.** Pakistan's clearing-and-settlement rating
   stayed at the bottom band after T+1, for reasons T+1 does not touch.
